The Core Thesis: Imperfection Is the Engine

Your own hunch was right — the 'ruined' postcard is the story. Scroll through why a flaw out-travels a masterpiece.

A like = the end

A 'perfect' postcard — a clean shot of the landmark — earns one thing: a like. Then the chain stops.

But a postcard where a giant Pikmin blocks the landmark? That earns a contest: 'who has the ugliest one?'

Why? An ugly shot is high-arousal funny (people share it), feels real (unedited), and is trivially imitable.

Perfect content's chain ends at 'admiration'. Imperfect content self-replicates: laugh → I'll make one too → a new flaw → laughed at again.

Five independent marketing frameworks — Berger, Sharp, Godin, transmission psychology, localization — all point to the same conclusion.

Perfection is where transmission ends. Imperfection is where it begins.
